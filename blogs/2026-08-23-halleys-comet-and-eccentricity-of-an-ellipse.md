# Halley's Comet & Eccentricity of Ellipses: A Thing about Meeting a Distant Forlorn Friend

**date:** `2026-08-23` `22:00` `UTC+5:30`  
**tags:** `math` `physics` `astronomy`

*Halley's Comet, photographed in 1986 by W. Liller.*

Eccentricity of an ellipse referes to how long and thin, or how circular, the shape is. It is defined by the ratio:

$$e = \frac{c}{a}$$

![Ellipse Labels](/assets/images/ellipse-labels.png)

If $e \approx 1$, the shape is long and thin. It's said to be more **eccentric**.

If $e \approx 0$, the shape is circular, and so less eccentric. 

Many celestial bodies orbit other bodies along elliptical paths. Planets in our solar system also revolve around the sun in elliptical orbits. The Earth does the same, although the orbit's eccentricity is so small that it's almost circular.

One very eccentric body in our system is **Halley's Comet** which has an **eccentricity of 0.98** and it's orbit around sun looks approximately like this:

![Orbit of Halley's Comet around the Sun](/assets/images/halleys-comet-orbit.png)

So it's there with the Sun and Earth for a very short time :)

It's orbit is about 7 astronomical unit wide by 35 astronomical unit long, where 1 astronomical unit (AU) = 150 million kilometers. **1 AU** is the average orbital distance between Sun and Earth (length of semi-major axis of Earth's orbit). At its closest approach, it is only 0.59 AU away from the Sun.

As it might seem from the picture, it takes a long time for Halley's Comet to complete a revolution around the Sun. It's period of revolution is 76 years. It was seen in 1910, and again in 1985-86. It will appear next in 2061.
